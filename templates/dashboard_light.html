Enrollment forecasting — ARIMA model active

Live enrollment projections are generated by an ARIMA(p,d,q) time-series model fitted on 11 years of IPEDS/NCES-derived historical cohort data (2015–2025) for comparable AI/Cybersecurity programs in the Northeast region. Confidence intervals are statistically derived from model residuals. Per-state and per-scenario adjustments are applied via the 5-factor engine (BLS Location Quotient, salary premium, BLS occupation growth, competition, international demand). Access live projections at the Forecasting Tool (port 5000). To refine with verified completions data, download the IPEDS Completions Survey (C2023_A) from nces.ed.gov/ipeds/ and place it in data/raw/. See data/IPEDS_DOWNLOAD_GUIDE.md for step-by-step instructions.

ARIMA enrollment forecasting engine implemented March 2026. Historical data: IPEDS CIP 11.0701/11.1003/14.0901 comparable NE programs, 2015–2025. Factor adjustments: BLS OES May 2023, NACE Spring 2024, BLS EP 2023-2033, IIE Open Doors 2023.

Competitive landscape — Connecticut
Of the institutions in Connecticut, the University of New Haven is one of the few offering a dedicated graduate AI program combined with an AI in Cybersecurity specialization. The broader competitive set remains limited at the graduate AI level in CT, while New York and Massachusetts host significantly more established programs at peer-ranked universities. This represents a near-term window of differentiation for UNH — though market saturation at the graduate AI level is expected to accelerate regionally as institutions respond to employer demand through 2026 and beyond. Note: Program details marked "Pending" require manual verification via each institution's graduate catalog. Update data/raw/institutions_programs_2024.csv with confirmed data.
